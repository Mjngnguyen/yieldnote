Life insurance exists to replace income your family depends on. That framing settles most of the term-versus-whole debate before it starts.

## How the two differ

- Term life covers a fixed period, usually 10–30 years, and pays only if you die in that window.
- Whole life covers you for life and builds a cash value, which makes premiums many times higher for the same payout.
- Term is pure protection. Whole life bundles protection with a conservative savings product.

## Who should pick term

If you have dependants, a mortgage or debts someone else would inherit, term usually fits. Choose a term that lasts until your youngest child is independent or the mortgage is paid off.

> **Tip:** A common starting point for cover is 10–12 times your annual income, adjusted for debts and savings you already have.

## When whole life can make sense

Permanent needs, such as a lifelong dependant or estate planning in a high-net-worth household, are the main cases. Get advice from a fee-only planner who does not earn commission on the policy.
